You probably came to Raku from another programming language. That is how
most people come to a second human language too: with a first one already
in their head, which helps a lot and sometimes gets in the way.

These articles teach Raku the way language teachers teach Dutch, Italian or
Latvian. There are no chapters on syntax here — the
[Raku course](https://course.raku.org) does that well. Instead, there are
four kinds of articles.

## Readers

A reader is a short, complete program at one level, from A1 to C2. You read
it before you run it and guess what it prints. Then you run it, check your
guess, and read the notes. New words are listed, as in a textbook for
learners.

Reading is how you learn a language fastest, and it is the one thing
programming courses almost never ask you to do.

[All readers →](/foreign-language/readers/)

## False friends

The Dutch word *bellen* means "to phone", not "bells". Programming
languages have false friends too: `a[-1]` is the last element in Python,
`$a[-1]` is the last element in Perl, and `@a[-1]` does not even compile in
Raku. Each article takes one false friend, shows what you expect from your
first language, and shows what Raku does instead.

[All false friends →](/foreign-language/false-friends/)

## Word families

`comb`, `words`, `lines` and `split` all cut a string into pieces. `x`,
`xx` and `X` all repeat or combine things. A word-family article takes one
group of relatives and shows what they share and where they differ, so you
learn them together and stop mixing them up.

[All word families →](/foreign-language/word-families/)

## Accents

You can write correct Raku that still sounds like Python. An accent article
takes one small task and writes it four times: with a Python accent, with a
Perl accent, with a C accent, and as a native speaker would. All four work.
Only one sounds like Raku.

[All accents →](/foreign-language/accents/)

## The rest of Learn Raku

Raku as a Foreign Language is one of six parts of [Learn Raku](/). The
other five:

- [A Tour of Raku](/tour/) — eighteen short lessons with live code;
- [Raku Drills](/drills/) — questions at six levels, from A1 to C2;
- [Raku by example](/examples/) — twenty-four complete programs to read
  and run;
- [The Raku Cookbook](/cookbook/) — whole tasks, worked end to end;
- [Raku Behind the Docs](/deep/) — what Raku actually does, corner by
  corner.

## Running the code

Every Raku example in these articles runs in your browser. Press **Run**,
change the code, and run it again. Nothing is sent to a server: the interpreter,
[Raku++](https://raku.online), runs on your machine.

## Where to start

Find your level on the [Levels](/foreign-language/levels/) page, and read
the articles at that level. Then read one level up. If you know Python,
Perl, JavaScript or C well, the
[pages for your language](/foreign-language/from/) list the false friends
and accents that start from it.
